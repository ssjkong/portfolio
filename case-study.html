Redesigning network intelligence

How surfacing anomalies before escalation reduced time-to-insight for distributed fleet management.

Problem
Finding

IT admins were reacting to fires instead of preventing them

Meraki's network monitoring dashboard had been built incrementally over years, resulting in an experience that surfaced raw data rather than actionable intelligence. Administrators managing hundreds of distributed devices were spending significant time diagnosing issues that had already escalated into outages.

The core problem: the system knew what was happening but didn't help users understand what it meant or what to do next. Every insight was buried two or three clicks deep.

"By the time the alert fires, the damage is already done. I need to see problems coming, not after the fact."

IT administrator, interview session 3 of 14
Process
Research

14 interviews across 4 organization sizes.

We mapped the full mental model of how IT administrators understand network health — what signals they trust, what they ignore, and how they decide when something needs attention. The pattern across organization sizes was consistent: admins had built personal workarounds because the product didn't support their actual workflow.

Prototyping

Three approaches to surfacing anomalies.

We explored three directions with different philosophies on how to surface intelligence: a timeline-first approach, a topology-first approach, and a triage-queue model. Each was tested at low fidelity before committing to higher-resolution prototypes.

Testing

Validated with 22 participants across 3 rounds.

The triage-queue model consistently outperformed the alternatives — not because it was the most comprehensive, but because it matched how experienced administrators already thought. It gave names to the things they were already doing.

"This is how I think about my network. I just didn't think software could think that way too."

Senior network administrator, usability test round 2
Outcomes
Results

Shipped across enterprise and SMB tiers. Measured over 60 days.

The redesigned intelligence layer launched with a phased rollout across fleet sizes. Support ticket volume, time-to-resolution, and NPS were tracked as primary signals.

Key results — 60-day post-launch
−60%
Reduction in time-to-insight for anomaly detection
−34%
Support tickets related to missed alerts
+28
Net Promoter Score points among power users

The broader organizational impact: the intelligence-first framing became a shared design principle applied to three subsequent feature areas by other teams, and was incorporated into the design system as a pattern category.
